# 2026-10-25 - Finding the attack, and snapping to zero crossings

The user asked whether the JSFX could scan the samples to tell which part is
the transient and which is the body, and whether there could be an option to
snap to zero crossings. Both are in, with decisions 0012 and 0013.

The 2026-10-15 work had been merged into `main` (pull request #1) and its
branch deleted. `claude/loving-carson-xcyc30` was restarted from `main`,
as a merged branch must not carry new work.

## The route

- **Finding the attack, in Python first.** Six made-up sounds were built
  with a known click-and-body split: a kick with a click, an 808, a snare, a
  hat, a pluck and a slow pad.
  - **Level alone:** a fast follower of the level against a slow one, the
    usual transient-designer measure. Right for the snare and hat, but a
    kick came out at 47 ms and a pluck at 69 ms: the louder body's own decay
    hid the click.
  - **Brightness alone:** the sound above 2.5 kHz fading 20 dB. Right for
    the kick and the pluck, but the snare came out at 176 ms, because its
    wires stay bright.
  - **The earlier of the two** was right everywhere it should be, and found
    nothing for the pad. That became `find_attack_end`.
- **Ported to the JSFX** with the same numbers. In the plug-in: pluck 9 ms,
  kick 6 ms, snare 12–14 ms, swell none, the same as in Python. Each result
  splices where its own attack sample's attack ends, so each result got its
  own splice (`s_att`, `s_xf`, `s_s0`) instead of one shared one.
- **The picture showed a fault the checks didn't.** With a found 7 ms attack
  and a 10 ms crossfade centred on it (as 0002 had it), the fade began 2 ms
  after the hit and blended away the click it had just found. The crossfade
  now starts at the attack length, found or set by hand (0012, with a
  pointer added to 0002).
- **Snapping.** Two sounds meet at the splice, so a zero crossing of one is
  rarely one of the other. The snap looks within 5 ms for the point where
  both are nearest zero together, each against its own level, with the body
  shifted by Align phase's lag (0013). On the test pair it moved the splice
  3.5 ms, and how far the two were from zero dropped from 2.7 to 0.27 on the
  test's scale.
- **The window.**
  - *Find attack* and *Snap to zero crossings* are two new checkboxes, made
    with `ui_toggle`, so they get Tab, a focus ring and hints for free.
  - The controls now take three rows; the window opens at 820 × 704.
  - Attack length says "7.0 ms, found", or "31.3 → 29.9 ms" when snapping
    moved the splice.
  - Moving it, or dragging the line, turns Find attack off.
  - A mark at the bottom of the A and B rows shows where each sample's
    attack ends.
- **Checks: 677 → 797.** The measured values were printed once, to be sure
  the new checks really tell sounds apart, and they matched the prototype.

## What looked wrong and wasn't

- **Every new check passed on the first run.** Printing the numbers showed
  they genuinely tell the cases apart: 6 and 9 ms against limits of 2–15,
  and snapping cutting the distance from zero by ten times.
- **The 808 in the pictures shows an attack-end mark at 7 ms.** The preview's
  808 has a faint click (0.05) mixed in, and that is what was found.

## Not done yet

- **Find attack hasn't heard a real sample.** It is tuned on made-up sounds.
  A flam, a reversed sample, or a sample with noise before the hit may need
  Hit threshold adjusting, or Find attack turned off.
- **No way to move one sample's attack-end mark by hand.** You can only turn
  Find attack off and set one Attack length for both.
- **Snapping uses mono** (left plus right).
- Everything in the 2026-10-15 log's list, especially the REAPER behaviours
  the tests can't reach.
